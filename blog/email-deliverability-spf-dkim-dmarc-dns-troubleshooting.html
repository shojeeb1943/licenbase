Email Deliverability Problems: SPF, DKIM, DMARC & DNS Troubleshooting

By LicenBase Team Published November 9, 2026 5 min read

Few technical failures impact business operations as severely as email deliverability breakdown. When legitimate invoices, password reset tokens, and sales proposals vanish into client spam folders or trigger instant 550 bounce rejections, commercial revenue stalls. With global mailbox providers like Google, Yahoo, and Microsoft enforcing strict sender authentication thresholds, mastering SPF, DKIM, DMARC, and reverse DNS (PTR) troubleshooting is no longer optional—it is mandatory.

1. The Anatomy of Modern Email Authentication

To thwart spoofing, phishing, and ransomware impersonation, modern mail transfer agents execute an automated four-stage verification gauntlet before admitting any incoming message to the user's primary inbox:

  • SPF (Sender Policy Framework): A public DNS TXT record listing all IP addresses and third-party relays authorized to transmit email on behalf of your domain.
  • DKIM (DomainKeys Identified Mail): An asymmetric cryptographic signature added to message headers. The receiving MTA fetches your public key from DNS to confirm the message body was not altered in transit.
  • DMARC (Domain-based Message Authentication, Reporting & Conformance): An orchestration policy informing receiving servers what to do if SPF or DKIM fails (none, quarantine, or reject), while generating diagnostic feedback reports.
  • Reverse DNS (PTR Record): Verifies that the public IP address of the sending server matches its forward fully qualified domain name (FQDN).

2. Troubleshooting SPF Errors & the 10-Lookup Limit

The most pervasive SPF flaw is the RFC 7208 10-DNS-lookup limit. When businesses add multiple third-party marketing tools (such as Google Workspace, Zendesk, Mailchimp, and Shopify) into a single SPF string, each include: directive triggers recursive DNS requests. If the total query count exceeds 10, receiving MTAs return PermError and flag the email as suspect.

Inspect your SPF record using command-line diagnostic tools:

dig TXT yourdomain.com +short

A properly hardened SPF syntax should consolidate authorized IPs directly and terminate with a hard fail mechanism:

"v=spf1 ip4:198.51.100.25 include:_spf.google.com -all"

Notice the use of -all (hard fail) rather than ~all (soft fail). Leading enterprise email providers increasingly downgrade soft-fail messages when spoofing indicators are present.

3. Resolving DKIM Selector and Key Mismatches

A DKIM failure indicates that the receiving server could not validate the cryptographic hash in the message's DKIM-Signature header. Common root causes include:

  • Mismatched Key Length: Modern mail systems reject legacy 1024-bit RSA keys. Ensure your mail server generates 2048-bit DKIM keys.
  • Incorrect DNS Selector: The selector specified in the header (e.g., s=default) must match the exact DNS record location: default._domainkey.yourdomain.com.
  • String Splitting Corruption: Because 2048-bit keys exceed 255 characters, some DNS managers truncate the string. Ensure your TXT record properly concatenates multiple quoted strings without inserting errant whitespace.

Verify your public DKIM record with dig:

dig TXT default._domainkey.yourdomain.com +short

4. DMARC Policy Staging & Alignment Best Practices

DMARC bridges SPF and DKIM through alignment. Alignment requires that the domain in the visible 'From' header matches the domain authenticated by SPF and/or DKIM. Do not configure a strict rejection policy on day one. Follow a staged migration path:

  1. Stage 1 (Monitoring): v=DMARC1; p=none; rua=mailto:dmarc-reports@yourdomain.com; — Gathers aggregate telemetry without impacting message delivery.
  2. Stage 2 (Quarantine): v=DMARC1; p=quarantine; pct=25; rua=mailto:dmarc-reports@yourdomain.com; — Routes suspicious traffic from 25% of unauthenticated senders to spam folders while monitoring legitimate edge cases.
  3. Stage 3 (Full Enforcement): v=DMARC1; p=reject; rua=mailto:dmarc-reports@yourdomain.com; — Blocks 100% of unauthorized impersonation attempts at the gateway.

5. Reverse DNS (PTR) and Server Reputation Hardening

Even with immaculate SPF, DKIM, and DMARC records, messages will bounce if your sending server IP lacks a matching reverse DNS (PTR) record. Forward-confirmed reverse DNS (FCrDNS) requires that:

IP 198.51.100.25  --> resolves to mail.yourdomain.com (PTR)
mail.yourdomain.com --> resolves to 198.51.100.25 (A Record)

If you host transactional notifications on an independent server, ensure your infrastructure provider supports customizable rDNS, such as our port 25 open VPS or managed VPS instances. Furthermore, protect your server from outbound spam infections using an automated Imunify360 license or cPanel license with automated outbound rate limiting.
